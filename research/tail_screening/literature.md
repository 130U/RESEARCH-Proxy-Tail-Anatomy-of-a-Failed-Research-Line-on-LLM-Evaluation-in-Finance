# What does a cheap model miss in the tail: prior-art check and the question that can be kept

2026-11-16. Independent literature check of the second-version direction from the same day.

**The current conclusion is to narrow the claim and continue testing; novelty cannot be declared yet.** There are already papers that model the confidence of LLMs of different tiers with copulas and optimize cascades, and there is already theory that directly interprets the tail dependence between prediction scores and the target as the ability to predict extreme events. SUPG and later semantic-filtering work have already solved the threshold design problem of "screening with a cheap proxy, a small amount of expensive labeling, reaching a recall target". So "introducing dependence into LLM cascades", "explaining extreme misses with tail dependence" and "recall-constrained proxy screening" cannot be contributions on their own.

Among the relevant original papers opened in this check, we have not found a complete study that takes **continuous semantic scores of LLMs of different tiers on financial text** as its object and at the same time studies **the pass-through fraction at fixed recall as the target tail fraction shrinks**, the difference between the positive and negative tails, and out-of-sample prediction. This is only a bounded search result, not proof that no such work exists. In particular, the full text of the 1984 paper on selection by proxy ranking has not been obtained, so the prior-art check is not fully closed.

## 1. Retrieve-then-rerank: the candidate set size and the interaction of the two stages have long been studied

Zamani, Bendersky, Metzler, Zhuang and Wang, *Stochastic Retrieval-Conditioned Reranking* (ICTIR 2022), §3.1 describes the standard structure in which low-cost retrieval produces a candidate set and expensive reranking processes it; the ECR objective in §4 is directly conditional on the first-stage retrieval results and analyzes the relation between reranking noise and the candidate pool; the paper then proposes randomized sampling and learnable truncation. The key difference is that it optimizes the final ranking metric, and when the reranker has errors, enlarging the candidate pool can also introduce interference, so the overall gain cannot be judged by first-stage recall alone. In the modeling and method sections examined here, it does not propose a fixed-recall widening exponent under Laplace conditional extremes. If our work continues, it should state clearly that it studies only "whether the top of the reference model can reach the next stage"; final answer quality, returns and reranking errors are different objects. [Authors' full text, §§3–4](https://maroo.cs.umass.edu/getpdf.php?id=1452), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3539813.3545141).

This branch also needs to acknowledge an older mathematical entry point: observing the target's **concomitants of order statistics** after ranking by a proxy. Yeo and David, *Selection through an Associated Characteristic, with Applications to the Random Effects Model* (JASA 79, 399–405, 1984), is a direct object of the prior-art check. Its bibliographic details in the journal have been verified; the publisher's full text returned 403 this time, so this document does not claim to have ruled out that it contains the same selection-scale asymptotics. One cannot bypass this literature after searching only "LLM tail dependence". [Journal table of contents](https://www.tandfonline.com/toc/uasa20/79/386), [original DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1984.10478063).

A final targeted follow-up search was done the same day: four queries combining the exact title with PDF, the Iowa State institutional repository and the authors' affiliations; in addition, the publisher's public PDF/full-text entry and the [JSTOR record of the original](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2288282) were checked directly. Still no author/institution full text that can be read directly was found, and the journal entry also returned no text. The search stopped there; the authors were not contacted and access restrictions were not bypassed. **The full text was not obtained, so we cannot judge whether that paper already gives a rare-tail screening-width law at fixed recall; nor can the citations or abstracts found be treated as a completed prior-art check.**

Khorrami Chokami and Kratz, *On the relation between extremal dependence and concomitants* (2023, arXiv:2301.08856), already connects extreme dependence with selection by proxy ranking: §1 defines the observations of the other dimension paired by the order of one dimension, §§2–4 analyze the maxima of selected and unselected concomitants, and the Gaussian case is studied specifically. Its target is the joint limit of the maxima of two subsets, which is not the minimum pass-through fraction under fixed-share recall; but it rejects the broad novelty claim "extreme dependence used for proxy ranking". [Original PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08856).

## 2. Personnel selection: the Gaussian baseline is an old tool, and non-Gaussian extensions are not a blank either

Taylor–Russell (1939) links the population success rate, the selection ratio, the correlation coefficient and the success rate among those selected, in a bivariate normal model. Thomas–Owen–Gunst (1977) extend it to multiple cutoffs. The CRAN page can be used to check the implementation of the corresponding bivariate/multivariate normal integrals and the inverse solution for thresholds; it is the software author's description and cannot replace reading the original papers. These results are enough to show that the Gaussian overlap curve and the numerical inversion of thresholds should be reproduced as a baseline, not presented as a new theory. [CRAN author's description](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/personnelSelectionUtility/vignettes/taylor-russell-and-thomas-owen-gunst.html), [Taylor–Russell DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0057079), [Thomas–Owen–Gunst DOI](https://doi.org/10.3102/10769986002001055).

Raju, Steinhaus, Edwards and DeLessio, *A Logistic Regression Model for Personnel Selection* (Applied Psychological Measurement 15(2), 139–152, 1991), already describe the success probability with logistic regression and handle selection, range restriction and utility, so "dropping the bivariate normal assumption" cannot itself be treated as an innovation. This time the publisher's abstract and the first page of the university archive were readable, but access to the full text was unstable; on this basis its model cannot be judged directly to duplicate a tail-exponent law. [Journal abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/014662169101500204), [university archive](https://conservancy.umn.edu/bitstream/handle/11299/114330/v15n2p139.pdf).

What this branch requires of new work: give **a scaling relation in rarity with testable error**, rather than writing "the correlation is not enough" once more or a non-Gaussian selection table. One also has to distinguish fixed recall in population probability from the probability that all of the top-k in a finite population are selected; the two should not be mixed.

## 3. Extreme-value prediction and LLM score dependence: the novelty boundary that most needs tightening

Heffernan–Tawn, *A conditional approach for multivariate extreme values* (JRSS B 66(3), 497–546, 2004), establishes the normalized model of conditional extremes; §8.6, journal pp.527–528, explicitly derives the Gaussian case, where under positive correlation the location normalization is $`\rho^2 x`$ and the scale $`x^{1/2}`$. So $`\alpha=\rho^2`$ is an existing result, not a discovery of this project. The original uses Gumbel margins; the signed Laplace-version expressions $`\alpha=\rho^2\mathrm{sign}(\rho)`$, $`\beta=1/2`$ can be checked in Kereszturi–Tawn–Jonathan (2016), author manuscript p.10. [HT journal PDF, §8.6](https://www.d.umn.edu/~yqi/mydownload/heffemantawn.pdf), [author manuscript with the Laplace expressions](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/~jonathan/KrsTwnJnt15.pdf).

Turning this conditional distribution into a pass-through probability is this project's inference from and interpretation of the existing model. Whether the conclusion the assumptions allow is $`q_\gamma(p)=p^{\alpha+o(1)}`$, ratio equivalence, or a finite-threshold formula with a controlled remainder must be proved separately; the three cannot all be written as "approximately equal". Lugrin–Tawn–Davison, *Sub-asymptotic motivation for new conditional multivariate extreme models* (Stat 10, e401, 2021), §3.1, Theorem 1 and formulas (12)–(13), already study the finite-threshold normalization correction for the Gaussian, including a $`\log(u)/u`$ term, and show slow convergence. A blanket claim of "giving the second-order correction for the first time" also does not hold. [Journal full text](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/sta4.401).

Verma–Stoev–Chen, *On the Optimal Prediction of Extreme Events in Heavy-Tailed Time Series With Applications to Solar Flare Forecasting* (2025 online; JTSA 47(1), 25–42), §2.1 allows the alarm fraction to differ from the event fraction, §2.2 and Corollary 2.1 give the optimal prediction for the Gaussian copula, and §2.3 explicitly identifies the limiting precision of equal-fraction alarms with the tail dependence coefficient. Bobbia–Stoev, *On the optimal prediction of extreme events* (arXiv:2606.26270v2, 2026), §§2.2–2.3 also give results under Archimedean/Gumbel copulas, and §3 studies optimal extreme prediction in a regular variation framework. The main asymptotic object of both is the limiting precision of equal-fraction prediction; this differs from the inverse problem of fixed recall with a variable pass-through fraction, but it already covers the core interpretation "tail dependence is the ability to predict extremes". [Full text of the former, §§2.1–2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11887v2), [full text of the latter, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26270v2).

The existing paper closest to LLMs is Zellinger–Thomson, *Rational Tuning of LLM Cascades via Probabilistic Modeling* (arXiv:2501.09345v3, 2025). §3.1 models the joint confidence of the model tiers with a Markov structure; §3.2 uses margins with atoms and a Gumbel copula; §4.3.2 tests copula fits between OpenAI and other models. It has already done "dependence modeling → LLM cascade threshold optimization". Its object is mainly confidence in answer correctness, not extreme ranking of semantic scores on financial text; we did not find fixed-recall rare-tail widening curves in these sections. The paper also reports that on the MMLU validation set 45.7% of GPT-4o-mini's raw confidences are exactly 1, which shows that **an API providing logprobs does not automatically guarantee that the top can be strictly ranked**; this number cannot be extrapolated to the tie rate of current models or financial tasks. [Original paper, §§3.1–3.2, 4.3.2 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09345v3).

Multi-stage chains also already have conditional-extremes theory: Papastathopoulos–Casey–Tawn, *Hidden tail chains and recurrence equations for dependence parameters associated with extremes of stationary higher-order Markov chains* (2024 online; Advances in Applied Probability 57(2), 407–452, 2025), study normalization and recursions for dependence parameters in extreme states, and the Gaussian example in §5 again gives $`\alpha_i=\rho_i^2`$. A stationary Markov chain in time is not automatically the same as non-homogeneous model tiers, but "propagating extreme dependence along a chain" already has theory; a multiplicative relation across models must state its structural assumptions and the change in conditional distribution caused by screening. [Journal full text, especially §§3–5](https://doi.org/10.1017/apr.2024.47).

## 4. Database proxy screening: recall targets and cheap/expensive model combinations have already been done

Kang, Gan, Bailis, Hashimoto and Zaharia, *Approximate Selection with Guarantees using Proxies* (SUPG, PVLDB 13(11), 1990–2003, 2020), §3.2 directly sets up an expensive binary oracle, continuous proxy scores, a sampling budget and precision/recall targets; §§3.3–4 choose the proxy threshold by sampling. This is already the finite-population problem of "how wide must the net be cast to reach recall", and the paper also discusses rare positives. It mainly provides threshold estimation algorithms and probabilistic guarantees, not a conditional-extremes exponent for two continuous model scores. So its threshold-selection idea can be a direct comparison object, but it cannot be renamed as LLM screening and counted as a new problem. [Authors' original, §§3–4](https://ddkang.github.io/papers/2020/supg-paper.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.14778/3407790.3407804).

Zeighami–Shankar–Parameswaran, *Cut Costs, Not Accuracy: LLM-Powered Data Processing with Guarantees* (BARGAIN, arXiv:2509.02896v1, 2025), apply this kind of problem to LLM data processing. The recall-target query in §4.2 directly selects the largest proxy threshold that meets the recall target; §4.2.2 points out that uniform sampling is insufficient when positives are few, and uses positive density and adaptive sampling. So "small tail sample sizes make recall estimation hard" and "validating proxy recall with cheap/expensive OpenAI models" are not undeveloped areas either. Its binary task setting is still not the same as a study of continuous tails that change with the reference model's quantile. [Original paper, §4.2, §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02896v1).

Kim–Catheland–Ailamaki, *Fast LLM-Based Semantic Filtering: From a Unified Framework to an Adaptive Two-Phase Method* (arXiv:2606.08090v1, 2026), §§3–6 study accuracy constraints for semantic predicates, oracle soft confidence, proxy calibration and staged filtering; §7 gives the limit that the Bayes error rate places on oracle calls under its oracle probability model. It is highly relevant to budget savings and escalating hard cases, but its binary-predicate accuracy target is not the fixed-recall problem for the expensive model's top-$`p`$ quantile set. The *Is Escalation Worth It?* (2605.06350) was retitled in v2 on 2026-10-05 as *Is Escalation Worth It? On the Depth of LLM Cascades*: §3 studies cascade thresholds, costs and expected accuracy gains under joint scores, and its focus is also not rare semantic tops. The version used should be stated when citing it. [Semantic Filtering full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08090v1), [Cascade Depth v2 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06350v2).

## Assessment

The existing evidence already rejects the broad novelty narrative, but it has not yet triggered the full rejection condition "the same law with the same LLM measurement has been found". The minimal question worth keeping is: **on a pre-fixed financial semantic scoring task, can the pass-through rate needed for a cheap model to screen back the reference model's extreme set be predicted on an independent sample by a conditional-extremes model; and is there a stable difference between the two tails that matters for cost?** The expensive model here is the reference ranking and cannot be called objective truth; the consistency of the reference ranking also cannot directly imply investment returns.

Mathematically, merely inverting an existing conditional-extremes model into a power of $`p`$ is very likely not enough to support a theory paper on its own. A weightier goal, not yet achieved, is to prove sufficiently weak and checkable conditions, make explicit how finite-threshold error affects the fixed-recall budget, and show that such predictions beat the exact Gaussian baseline and a direct empirical threshold on new data. Particular care is needed not to confuse $`\alpha`$ with Ledford–Tawn's $`\eta`$ or the equal-threshold tail dependence coefficient $`\chi`$ as the same quantity.

The most discriminating practical next step is still the small-sample score-resolution test and the direct widening curves at finite $`p`$. If the scores are usable but only reproduce the existing Gaussian relation, the work should be closed out as an empirical rule; if a stable deviation appears, ties, the prompt, topic mixing and reference-model error should be tested first, before discussing an extreme-dependence mechanism.

This search relied mainly on the journal versions, author manuscripts and arXiv full texts of the four literature branches; it focused on combinations such as "tail dependence / conditional extremes / concomitants / retrieval-conditioned reranking / recall-target proxy selection / copula LLM cascade", and traced the citations of the closest work. It does not claim to have completed an exhaustive systematic review. The access gaps for the Yeo–David original, the original full text of Taylor–Russell and some university archives are all kept as limitations and cannot be replaced by "not found in search".
