## Closest work and source evidence

The short quotations below are needed to pin down locations; the total English quotation per source does not exceed 25 words. XBRL is a near neighbour shared by both candidates and is excerpted only once below.

### Candidate 1: three near neighbours

**1A. Thomas A. Gittings, 1991, Rounding errors and index numbers. Source level: full text.** Chicago Fed *Economic Perspectives* May/June, printed pages 2–10; full text read.

> "Rounding of historical index numbers can distort the pattern of monthly growth rates" (p.2)

The object of study is rebased indices such as CPI-W: earlier values are small and their last-digit unit is relatively coarse, so monthly growth rates can be distorted substantially by rounding; the full text contains examples with actual indices and simulated time series. It confirms that the risk of rounding in growth rates was studied long ago. It does not give an input-precision audit of a FinQA-type benchmark, and no validation of "a difference of about 100 last-digit units" on company report data was seen. One cannot say this project is the first to find that growth rates are affected by rounding. [Full text](https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/economic-perspectives/1991/ep-may-jun1991-part1-gittings-pdf.pdf)

**1B/2A. XBRL International, Calculations 1.1, Recommendation 2023-02-22, corrected version 2024-02-14. Source level: specification text.** §§3–5.7 read.

> "the calculation is performed using the intersection of fact value intervals for all facts within the set." (§5.5)

§5.2.3 gives a closed interval of half a last-digit unit on each side of the displayed value; §5.7 gives interval addition and multiplication by weights; §5.2 checks consistency by whether the summed interval intersects the total's interval. The two disclosures in §5.5 are 5000 (decimals=-3) and 5500 (decimals=-2), with intervals [4500,5500] and [5450,5550] and intersection [5450,5500], which shows a quantitative tightening effect. There is no experiment on an overall non-identifiability rate in financial QA or on growth-rate thresholds, but it **already meets candidate 2's broad boundary**. [Specification](https://www.xbrl.org/Specification/calculation-1.1/REC-2023-02-22+corrected-errata-2024-02-14/calculation-1.1-REC-2023-02-22+corrected-errata-2024-02-14.html)

**1C. Bethel Hall, Sachi Shome, William Eiers, 2026, VeriFin: A Neurosymbolic Framework for Verifying LLM-Generated Financial Claims. Source level: preprint full text, v3 (2026-10-23).** §§1–6 read, especially §§3.5–3.6 and 4.1–4.3.

> "The tolerance τ is half a unit in the last place reported by the filing" (§3.6)

Its formulation binds each input to the disclosed point value, computes the expected result and then compares it with the candidate answer, using a tolerance of half a last-digit unit. The evaluation covers 600 XBRLFiling questions, 67 FinanceBench questions and 403 TAT-DQA questions; the outcome measures are the correctness and coverage of claim verification. This design does not treat each input as an unknown pre-rounding variable, and does not propagate input intervals to count target non-identifiability. It is very close as an application of "disclosure precision determines the verification tolerance" and must be discussed; one cannot say in general that nobody in financial QA has dealt with reported precision. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10213v3)

### Candidate 2: two further near neighbours (the first is 2A above)

**2B. Ramesh A. Dandekar, 2010, (In)Effectiveness of Independent Rounding of Discrete Tabular Data as Statistical Disclosure Control Strategy. Source level: conference paper full text.** ASA JSM Survey Research Methods, pp.1158–1164; the full text and the appended presentation pages were read.

> "The LP setup uses publicly available rounding rules in combination with additive properties associated with the table structure." (p.1161)

The paper uses a hypothetical 5×4 count table and the public CTPP rounding rules to build upper and lower bounds and row/column sum constraints (pp.1159–1162); p.1163 minimises and maximises each variable in turn to find the possible range of the pre-rounding values, and judges exact recovery by the bounds being equal. Table 6 shows an LP reconstruction example. This is statistical disclosure protection, not company filings or FinQA, and the example cannot be described as an overall recovery rate for real companies; but it directly covers the methodological skeleton of "rounding bounds + linear redundancy + LP recovery". [Original conference paper](http://www.asasrms.org/Proceedings/y2010/Files/306584_56766.pdf)

**2C. Juan-José Salazar-González, 2005, A Unified Mathematical Programming Framework for Different Statistical Disclosure Limitation Methods. Source level: journal article full text (typeset version hosted on a university website).** *Operations Research* 53(5):819–829, DOI 10.1287/opre.1040.0202; §§1–2 and 5–6 read.

> "Otherwise, yi is a continuous variable and the models are linear programs (LP)." (p.821, §2; variable typesetting converted to plain text)

§2 uses linear sum relations and per-cell upper and lower bounds to find the minimum/maximum possible values of suppressed numbers, distinguishing continuous LP from integer ILP; §5 deals specifically with controlled rounding and allows bounds formed from the published values and the rounding rules. Fig.1 is a table of business investment by region/activity, which is not the same as a QA audit of listed companies. It shows that using constraints and extremal programming for information recovery already has a mature framework. [Full text](https://www.cs.utep.edu/vladik/cs5354.10/819.pdf)

## Additional near neighbours and limits on the write-up

In [Yan, Miao & Huang 2026, Training Autoregressive Language Models on Rounded, Bounded, and Censored Numerical Labels via Exact Likelihood](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02461), full text §3.2 and Table 1 already treat rounded labels as compatible intervals and train an exact likelihood over the whole set. The experiments use synthetic data, hERG and code latency; no FinQA/TAT-QA, and no gold set obtained by propagation from filing operands, was seen. **"A rounded label should be a set" cannot be presented as a concept unique to this project**; the remaining space for candidate 1 is the input-level identifiability audit and its specific empirical conclusions. Source level: full text (including Appendix D/E).

[XFinBench](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/findings/2025.findings-acl.457.pdf) (2025) §4.4/F.1 analyses rounding in the model's intermediate steps; [Retrieval-Augmented Chain-of-Thought in Semi-structured Domains](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/nllp/2023.nllp-1.18.pdf) (2023) §5.1 discusses how scoring ignores differences in precision and rounding. The relevant full-text and appendix passages were read; neither can be used to prove or refute the input-identifiability claim. A keyword hit on precision or rounding does not mean the same object of study.

The earlier [XBRL Precision, Decimals and Units RFC, 2008](https://www.xbrl.org/rfc/pdu/pwd-2008-10-09/pdu-rfc-pwd-2008-10-09.html) §§6–9 already discusses interval consistency, and §9 mentions the practice of adjusting displayed numbers to keep sums balanced. So the half-last-digit interval should be stated explicitly as an assumption of the rounding model; one cannot assert without verification that every number in every filing is independently rounded to nearest. Source level: full text of a specification discussion draft.
